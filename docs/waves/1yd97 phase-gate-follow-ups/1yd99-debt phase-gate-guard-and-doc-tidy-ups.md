## Rationale

Wave `1y0h0`'s delivery review approved with two findings repaired. Its lanes also carried a set of low and informational observations that were never written to a durable artifact: the ledger records only the two findings, and the delivery-review note records none. An earlier draft of this change asserted that the count was unrecoverable. That was wrong, and it was wrong because the ledger was not searched rather than because the record is absent: the delivery council synthesis in `events.jsonl` states it, recording that nine further low or informational observations are carried as follow-ups, none of them a landed-behavior defect. All nine are individually nameable from the delivery-phase records, once those records are read past the approval events: seven from the qa and architecture approvals, and two more from the red-team reverification record's limitations field. The predicate is every carried observation that is not RT-F1 or RT-F3, taken from the records whose `approval_phase` is the delivery review together with the reverification records recorded in that same run. The field matters: every record in that ledger carries `phase: delivery`, including the readiness-stage approvals, so keying on `phase` would sweep in the readiness notes those same lanes carried and yield a population far larger than the nine being reconciled. Requirement 10 maps each one onto the requirement, Scope exclusion or recorded disposition that discharges it, so the completeness claim rests on a mapping rather than on an assertion. An earlier draft of this requirement declared two of the nine unnameable and prescribed a residual row saying so; that was the same error a second time, an assertion of absence made without searching every record class. None is a landed-behavior defect, and several were deliberately not fixed inside that wave because the production scope was frozen behind an eight-path hash: touching any of those files would have invalidated the completed independent code-review verdict for a cosmetic change. This change collects them now that the freeze has served its purpose. The ten requirements below are the complete set this change claims.

Two are worth more than tidiness. The boundary scanner misses a module rebind, so a future edit could bind a moved helper through an alias and defeat the attribute-binding invariant the layering row claims, while every landed check stays green. And the lifecycle golden self-heals: with the regeneration variable exported in a shell or CI job, drift is rewritten into the committed fixture and the test passes. The second golden is a working backstop today, which is why neither blocked, but a guard that can be silently disarmed is worth closing while the context is fresh.

## Requirements

1. The boundary scanner resolves module aliases established by assignment, not only by import, so binding the support module to a local name and reading a helper off it is caught the same as a from-import. The fixpoint form the facade scanner already uses for ledger names is the precedent.
2. The lifecycle golden refuses to regenerate over an existing committed fixture unless a second explicit flag is set, so an ambient regeneration variable cannot bake drift into the fixture and report success. The refusal keys on fixture existence, so the landed known-bad test that regenerates a not-yet-existing temp fixture keeps passing. The module docstring of `tests/test_lifecycle_golden.py` currently states that regeneration happens under the environment variable alone; this change falsifies that sentence, so correcting it to name both flags is part of the requirement rather than left to whoever notices. The tool-surface golden carries the byte-identical self-heal with no frozen twin, and is deliberately excluded here: its documented workflow sanctions environment-variable regeneration, and changing that is a separate decision rather than a tidy-up.
3. Two corrections in two different functions, named separately because one has a dangerous lookalike. In `_approval_evidence_diagnostics`, the second guard's `wave_md is None` conjunct is dead, unreachable after the identical test in the guard immediately above it, and is removed. In `_review_evidence_diagnostics`, the absent-argument message that still says "wave key" is reworded to name the record path the parameter now takes. That second function also contains a superficially similar conjunct, the `not result.errors` test guarding the projection comparison, which is **load-bearing** and must not be touched: it is what keeps the comparison from reading a local that is bound only inside the branch above it, so deleting it raises on every path where the ledger validator reported errors.
4. A dedicated canonical constant is named in `review_evidence` and the marker is repointed to it at all five reading sites, which carry seven subscript expressions in total, because each of the two configuration-default sites reads it twice, once as the `.get` default and once as the `or` fallback for a value that is empty after stripping. The two options are not interchangeable and only this one removes the coupling: with a named constant, prepending to the marker tuple changes no remedy message and no configuration default. The constant must live in `review_evidence` and be read through it, because the facade scanner rejects the literal section string inside either lifecycle module.
5. The documentation enumeration of responses that carry no provenance key, meaning no `configured_gates` key, is replaced by a derivation rule at every site in the two documents that states which responses lack it: the enumeration itself in `docs/architecture/cross-cutting-concerns.md` and in the `wf_prepare_wave` bullet of `docs/specs/mcp-tool-surface.md`, plus the `wf_review_wave` and `wf_close_wave` bullets in that same file, which state the narrower claim in unenumerated form and would otherwise be left contradicting the corrected bullet in the same release. The rule is stated over the producing layer rather than over call order, because one of its own members is returned after the handler was invoked and raised: **a response produced by a registration wrapper, or returned by a tool body before it calls its handler, carries no provenance key.** The scope is the three registered lifecycle tool bodies whose responses otherwise carry the key, `wf_prepare_wave`, `wf_review_wave` and `wf_close_wave`, together with the registration wrappers applied to them in `server_impl.py`, with the handler identified by the `wf_*_response(...)` call. The population is named rather than described, because the same argument-refusal construct appears in other registered wave tools and a derivation scoped to all of them would derive members the resolved set does not name. A registration wrapper here means one that returns a response of its own rather than the wrapped callable's result; three wrapping passes run today and two qualify, the third being the cost wrapper, which only forwards and from which the lifecycle tools are exempt anyway. The rule is followed by the set it currently resolves to, which adds the tool body's argument refusal and the publication guard's busy refusal to the three already named. An earlier wording, "every refusal returned before the handler is invoked", is rejected precisely because it excludes the publication-busy refusal that this change adds to the set. Stating the rule matters more than the list, because repairing an incomplete enumeration with a longer enumeration is the failure mode this wave's predecessor lost rounds to.
6. The `wf_mark_ac` deferred-criterion refresh call site of `_publish_prepare_policy_state` gains a distinct `except ProjectPublicationUnavailable` clause emitting the existing typed `project_publication_busy` diagnostic, which is non-advisory, rather than widening the existing `(OSError, ValueError)` tuple. The two call sites carry the identical narrow tuple but they are **not** symmetric, and the rule is deliberately stated over one of them rather than both. `wf_prepare_wave` is a registered publication writer whose registration wrapper already catches this exception and returns a typed busy envelope, so that site is covered and gains no inner clause: adding one there would shadow the wrapper, replacing a fail-fast refusal with an in-band diagnostic and letting prepare continue through the council and activation gates to its blocking early return. `wf_mark_ac` is absent from the publication writer registry, so its site is the one that raises unhandled today and the only one this change edits. Widening the tuple would be a regression at either site, re-labelling lock contention as a stale receipt. The reachability claim is corrected as well: the exception is rare because publication is normally uncontended and single-process, not unreachable, and it derives from `RuntimeError` so neither existing clause catches it.
7. The `isolated_run` docstring in `subprocess_util.py` is corrected, named by symbol because that module carries three docstrings describing the same capture behavior and only this one is wrong: it claims capture alone triggers UTF-8 decoding when the code keys on the text flag, a defect that cost this wave a carried implementation note and would mislead the next caller.
8. The `build_fixtures` docstring in `tests/test_lifecycle_golden.py` is corrected to state the distinction the fixtures actually carry. The symbol is named because several docstrings in that file describe the fixture set, and an unnamed target is satisfiable by correcting the wrong one. The inaccuracy is its claim that the extra lane on one fixture makes review and close reach the shared delivery evaluation, which asserts a distinction the captures do not show. Re-derived from the committed golden with the wave identifier normalized away: the review capture is identical across all three fixtures, two of them are identical at both close captures, and only prepare separates those two, while the third also separates at close. No two fixtures are byte-identical anywhere, because the identifier differs.
9. One carried observation is dispositioned rather than fixed: an architecture lane noted that the layering row's Verified column bundles criteria instead of mapping clause to criterion. The sibling change corrects that row's invariant, splits its execution clause by phase, and repoints its Verified column across all five resulting clauses with clause two and clause one's exclusivity marked inferred, which resolves it, so nothing is owed here beyond recording that it was neither dropped nor duplicated. The disposition is contingent on that repoint landing in the shape the sibling specifies; if that shape changes, this row is re-derived rather than assumed.
10. The carried observations are mapped rather than counted, and all nine reconcile. Seven come from the delivery approval records: from the qa lane, the fixture-docstring overstatement and the unreachable bare assertion; from the architecture lane, the marker-tuple ordering coupling, the dead guard conjunct, the stale wording in one diagnostic, the bundled layering citation, and the scanner alias gap. Two more come from the red-team reverification record's limitations field, which is a delivery-phase record and therefore in the predicate's population even though that seat wrote no approval event: a third producer of key-less responses in the tool body's argument refusal together with the upgrade-publication guard's second refusal shape, and a latent narrow except-tuple at one publication call site, unreachable at the time because the lock is re-entrant.

    Those last two discharge onto this change's own Requirements 5 and 6 respectively, which is why the change already carries their repairs. Implementation records a table with one row per observation, nine in all, giving it as the record puts it, the record it comes from, and the requirement, Scope exclusion or recorded disposition that discharges it. All nine are the floor: recording any of them as unrecovered is a failure, not a discharge, and no residual row is expected or accepted.
