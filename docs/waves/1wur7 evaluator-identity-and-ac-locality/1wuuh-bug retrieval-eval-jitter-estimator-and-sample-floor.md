## Rationale

`apply_baseline_comparison` derives a same-generation pair's run-to-run jitter from `warm_p95_ms` alone, a single near-max order statistic per run, and `_nearest_rank_p95` uses `rank = ceil(0.95 * n)`. Two consequences, both observed on wave `1wpif`'s evidence:

1. **The estimator is blind to a floor shift.** The `1wpif` pair reported `code_search` jitter of 2.6% while its median jitter was 18.1% and its minimum jitter 11.8% — understated 7.1 times. Two runs sharing generation, attempt, production digest, fixture digest and environment differed by 72 ms at the *minimum*, which only an external load can produce, and the contract could not see it. That misreport was then used, in good faith, as evidence that a contended measurement was the quietest in the wave, and it inverted a delivery conclusion until an independent paired A/B (delivered versus HEAD: +0.9 ms over 112 samples per arm) and an induced-contention control (+383 ms median, fully reversible) settled it.
2. **A "p95" over nine samples is the maximum.** `docs_search` has three applicable fixtures times three repetitions, so `warm_p95_ms` is literally `max(samples)` and is reported under a name that implies a tail quantile.

A gate whose reproducibility check cannot detect a contended run will keep promoting contended pairs as standing baselines, and a cross-generation comparison inherits the baseline pair's jitter, so one contended pair ratchets the latency band for every later wave.

## Requirements

1. Pair jitter SHALL be derived from the full warm-sample distribution (the sample floor and the median, not `warm_p95_ms` alone). The statistics SHALL be recorded in the per-tool `performance` block as `warm_floor_ms` and `warm_median_ms` beside `warm_p95_ms`, so the comparison stays self-contained and does not reach into `cases[].repetitions[]` (readiness CODE-RDY-8). Baselines predating this change lack those fields; that absence is what the deliberate re-baseline is for.
2. A pair whose floor or median jitter exceeds a declared threshold SHALL NOT be promotable as a standing baseline; the run SHALL report `operator_review_required` naming EVERY tool over the threshold, not one (readiness QA-RDY-7: in the recorded contended pair all four tools exceed any plausible threshold). The threshold SHALL be chosen from the recorded evidence rather than fitted: quiet pairs (`1seaw` baseline, `before-1seas`) top out at 1.7% floor and 1.6% median across all four tools, while the contended `1wpif` pair runs 11.8% to 36.7%, so any value in roughly the 3% to 10% band separates them with margin. The wiring SHALL be explicit. The `operator_review_required` VERDICT is reachable today through the measurement-phase path (absolute p95 and envelope ceilings, active kill switches); what is unreachable is the COMPARISON-DERIVED channel, because `apply_baseline_comparison`'s local list is never appended to and its second return value is discarded by the only production caller (readiness CODE-RDY-3, docs-contract DOCS-RDY-3). The change SHALL feed the comparison's reasons into that existing path before `_compute_run_id` and re-derive the verdict. It SHALL EXTEND the existing list object rather than reassign `report["operator_review_reasons"]`: that key is bound to the same object the verdict reads, so a reassignment yields a receipt that reports the regression while the verdict still says `pass`, which is the exact silent path this requirement exists to close (docs-contract lane, primer question 2 caveat).
3. A `warm_p95_ms` computed from fewer than a declared minimum warm-sample count SHALL be labelled as a small-sample estimate in the receipt. The consequence is LABELLING plus operator review, never silent removal of the clause: `docs_search` has exactly 9 warm samples permanently (3 applicable fixtures times `MEASURED_REPETITIONS`) and the corpus is out of scope, so a verdict-escalating reading would make a clean `pass` unreachable forever, which is the mirror image of the gate-shaping this wave forbids (readiness CODE-RDY-4). The declared minimum SHALL be stated relative to that standing n=9.
4. The latency clause SHALL be enforced as a hard violation EXACTLY when a production change is the only difference between the arms, which is `production_change_same_generation`. The other two kinds SHALL report it and route it to `operator_review_required`; in no mode is it silently dropped. AMENDED at delivery review (CODE-DEL-3, operator-approved 2026-09-01): the readiness wording kept hard enforcement on `same_generation_pair` as well. That is unsound — both arms share one production identity on one frozen index, so there is no change to attribute a regression to and any p95 difference is jitter by construction. Under the old p95-fed band the clause was structurally unreachable there; excluding p95 from the band (Requirement 1) made it reachable and turned it into a false-failure mode, with quiet-machine p95 swings of 1.79%, 10.70% and 22.90% on record against a 25% floor allowance. `cross_generation` routes for the original reason: inherited jitter describes the baseline machine state rather than the current one. See the Decision Log rows on gate-shaping and on CODE-DEL-3.
5. The change SHALL be landed, indexed, and re-baselined deliberately: the estimator change alters the evaluator digest, which the compatibility rule binds, so a fresh same-generation pair is recorded before it gates any ranking change.

## Scope

**Problem statement:** the standing gate's reproducibility check reads one near-max sample per run, so it cannot distinguish a quiet pair from a contended one.

**In scope:**

- `retrieval_eval.apply_baseline_comparison` jitter derivation, `_nearest_rank_p95` reporting and labelling, the `cross_generation` latency posture, receipt fields that carry the new statistics, tests, and the `review-and-evals.md` contract prose.

**Out of scope:**

- Metrics, floors, corpus, or fixture changes; the store-identity binding owned by `1wtpl`; any production retrieval change.
