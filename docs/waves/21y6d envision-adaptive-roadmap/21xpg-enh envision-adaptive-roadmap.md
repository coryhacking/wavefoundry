## Rationale

The operator wants an ambitious product vision that includes a credible roadmap for reaching it over long horizons, through technical and nontechnical work. Wave 21tpp already supplies destination, beneficiary value, foundations, useful horizons and adaptation. This follow-up improves presentation, proportionality, treatment of distant uncertainty and continuity into later planning. It does not replace the roadmap with an inspirational statement or make enabling foundations optional.

The current source requires dependencies, critical path and timing assumptions, but does not explicitly reduce precision with distance or organize nontechnical prerequisites as roadmap workstreams. The current Plan change briefing does not name a vision-to-change handoff. These are observed instruction gaps; resulting agent behavior remains unverified until the new focused trials. The preceding comparison accepted both prompt versions, so it did not demonstrate comparative superiority or establish that the extra 776 words caused better outputs.

Target delivery before the next release, with 21tpp closed before this change opens for implementation. This preserves its frozen candidate, approvals and evaluation. The reported absence of seed179 from released versions is a scheduling premise to recheck against local release artifacts at implementation; do not imply that a hypothetical upgrade probe is a real released-to-released transition.

## Product Intent

Product owners can grasp the ambitious destination from the opening, then use a proportionate roadmap to understand useful advances, technical and nontechnical prerequisites, consequential choices and uncertainty over time. Near work is actionable; distant work remains accountable without false precision. Subsequent change planning can explain how a bounded advance serves that direction while leaving ownership of vision revisions with Envision or the operator. AC-2 through AC-5 establish representative behavioral evidence; AC-1 and AC-6 establish the delivered instruction and distribution contracts. Neither proves future product success, general agent reliability or superiority to 21tpp.

## Requirements

1. Make the default new-document title `<Project>: Vision and Roadmap`; preserve existing titles and canonical locations unless the operator asks to change them. Lead with a short, repeatable destination statement in beneficiary language, followed by a concise roadmap overview before detailed rationale. A stakeholder should understand what becomes possible and why it matters from the opening alone.
2. Keep the roadmap core: work backward from the destination to foundations and prerequisites, then forward through useful horizons, dependencies, critical path, timing assumptions and conditions for advancing or reconsidering. Retain diagnosis, strategic choices/non-goals, useful intermediate value, the riskiest assumptions and the next bounded advance. Detail and precision decrease with distance: near horizons have concrete demonstrations and exit criteria; later horizons state intended outcomes, consequential assumptions and provisional advancement/reconsideration conditions, including evidence needed to firm them up. Qualitative conditions are valid. Do not invent distant numerical targets, dates or implementation commitments; retain justified operator-supplied timing as an explicit aspiration or constraint.
3. Make relevant nontechnical work first-class alongside engineering: research, adoption, distribution, funding, partnerships, operating practices and team capability can have milestones and prerequisites. Explain cross-workstream dependencies and count them toward the critical path. Do not force every listed track into every product. Nontechnical activity must connect to an outcome or uncertainty it resolves, not merely occupy a parallel checklist.
4. Scale authoring depth to stakes, maturity and uncertainty. Keep core reasoning intelligible for small internal tools and libraries without compulsory competitor studies or elaborate measurement machinery. Compare real alternatives where consequential, including existing manual practice. Apply advantage analysis, diagrams, unlimited-resource analysis and detailed evaluation designs only when they help the argument. Make independent evaluation conditional on the credibility needs of the claim, not exclusive to agentic products; evaluator/system separation remains specific guidance for agentic systems. Preserve evidence honesty, maintenance/adaptability and meaningful success/failure conditions.
5. Scale advisory review scope and depth to the same stakes, maturity and uncertainty, respecting operator direction. State the selected scope and expected effort in the existing consolidated briefing; do not force an eight-context full set merely because a small vision is new. Once a review form is selected, follow its canonical independence and execution protocol; report actual selected, omitted and unavailable coverage. Preserve explicit operator-requested full reviews and all artifact-only boundaries. Separate interview questions from review-scope announcements into readable paragraphs without adding another routine approval exchange. For a new or substantially revised vision, the default remains architecture, standalone red-team and Archetype Council; selecting less than that default requires a stated reason tied to the brief's stakes, maturity or uncertainty, so lower author effort alone never justifies narrowing. High-stakes or highly uncertain visions keep the default unless the operator directs otherwise. This changes Envision's advisory scope selection, not required lifecycle review policy or canonical council protocols.
6. Include the planning bridge in both workflows. Envision explains at delivery that subsequent planning identifies which horizon and outcome the operator-requested change advances and carries forward prerequisites and assumptions; it never selects work or redirects the request to manufacture alignment. Use the explicit ownership rule: **Delivery evidence informs horizon progress; recording it in the vision is an Envision revision or an explicit operator edit.** Met/missed exit conditions inform reconsideration; they neither silently change the destination nor authorize subsequent work. Only an adopted vision counts. Adoption means explicit project designation (for example a canonical vision location named in AGENTS.md or the project overview) or operator direction; Git tracking is neither required nor sufficient, so a designated but uncommitted vision counts and a committed draft does not. Plan change looks first for a designated vision, then falls back to root `VISION.md` for discovery only: a found but undesignated file is reported as available only when the request and already available project context make it plausibly relevant, not on every plan. Do not read the undesignated vision to establish that relevance; consult it only when the operator directs. Unadopted, personal or draft visions stay excluded. To bound context cost, start with the opening, roadmap overview and relevant horizon; follow only references needed to establish consequential dependencies, constraints or conflicts. Avoid reading the entire document by default. Plan change conditionally reads that vision and names the relevant horizon and outcome in its existing Product Intent or requirements, with consequential prerequisites/assumptions. Do not create a vision prerequisite, a new mandatory section, a tracker or an automatic write. A legitimate maintenance, safety or other change with no direct horizon fit states that briefly rather than inventing alignment; a conflict is exposed for operator decision. No vision means the normal planning workflow proceeds.
7. Deliver the same meaning through seed179/local Envision, seed170/local Plan change and discovery surfaces. Skill description/bullets and catalog copy explicitly describe the product vision and roadmap, preserving the thin-pointer design, host/doc gating, invocation policy and no-implementation-authority boundary. Reuse existing string/header and literal-phrase test patterns; add no YAML parser or general validator. Render active-host skills through the public renderer. Update architecture and release wording consistently. Because seed179 has not shipped in a release, fold the release wording into the existing Envision changelog bullet rather than adding a second bullet.
8. Reuse seed160's changed-seed179 reconciliation for Envision changes; extend its changed-seed170 carrier mapping to include the conditional planning bridge, with the equivalent local upgrade instruction. Preserve compatible project additions, existing vision titles/content and renderer-owned regions; stop ambiguous/conflicting merges rather than overwrite. Reconciliation never invokes Envision or writes the project's vision. Distinguish fresh prompt authoring from agent-executed existing-prompt reconciliation and executable skill rendering.
9. Evaluate the new claims directly, preserve every attempt and report limits. Do not reinterpret 21tpp's equal passing comparison as evidence of improvement. No arbitrary word ceiling or global trial-count ceiling; report prompt word counts and explain material growth. Predeclare finite batches and return evidence/options to the operator at their stopping point if a required outcome remains unproven or a repair fails its recheck, before another batch.
10. Make the authoring-to-planning adoption handoff explicit in Envision delivery guidance, outside the content-to-draft section. If the delivered vision is not designated, report that status and provide a concrete one-line canonical pointer for AGENTS.md or the project overview (for example `Canonical product vision and roadmap: VISION.md`, adjusted to the actual path). Do not write a designation without operator direction; preserve existing adoption when revising a designated vision. Keep adoption separate from authoring, review and Git tracking. Describe the path as technical and nontechnical advances, and consolidate repeated bridge ownership prose without weakening its read-only boundary.
